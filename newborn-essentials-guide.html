Newborn essentials: what you really need (and what can wait)

A calm, practical list for the first weeks, so you can buy what matters without overspending.

You do not need everything in the baby aisle. In the first weeks, a baby needs a safe place to sleep, milk, clean nappies, warm clothes and plenty of cuddles. Everything else is a bonus. Use this guide to buy what matters first, and wait on the rest.

This is general shopping advice, not medical advice. Your midwife or clinic may have their own list: follow it where it differs from ours.

1. A safe place to sleep

  • A cot, baby bed or Moses basket with a firm, flat mattress, and a fitted sheet or two.
  • Light blankets or swaddles that are easy to wash and dry.
  • Babies should sleep on their backs, on a firm flat surface, with nothing loose in the cot such as pillows, bumpers or soft toys.
  • If you swaddle, keep it snug around the chest but loose around the hips and legs, and stop once your baby starts trying to roll over.
  • In areas with mosquitoes, ask your clinic about a treated net for your baby's sleeping space.

2. Feeding

If you are breastfeeding, the basics are comfortable nursing bras (two or three), breast pads and a few burp cloths. A breast pump is optional: it helps if you will be away from baby or want someone else to do a feed, but many mums manage without one in the early weeks.

If you will use expressed milk or formula, you will need a few bottles with newborn teats, a bottle brush and a way to sterilise them (a pot of boiling water works). Ask your clinic before starting formula.

3. Clothes

  • 5 to 7 bodysuits (vests) and 4 to 5 sleepsuits.
  • 2 soft hats, a few pairs of socks or mittens, and a light cardigan.
  • Babies grow quickly, so buy a few newborn-size outfits and more in the 0 to 3 months size.
  • Wash everything in a gentle detergent before baby wears it.

4. Nappies, wipes and bathing

  • Nappies: a newborn can use 8 to 12 a day, so keep a good stock, but do not buy huge packs of the smallest size.
  • Wipes, or cotton wool and warm water, plus a changing mat.
  • A baby bath or tub, mild baby soap and soft towels.
  • Nappy cream only if your midwife or pharmacist recommends it.

5. Getting around

A baby carrier or wrap keeps your baby close and your hands free at home or on short errands. If you travel by car, a properly fitted car seat is important. It is the one item many parents choose to buy new, because a used seat may have damage you cannot see.

6. What can wait

  • Toys: newborns need very little beyond your face and voice.
  • Shoes and large piles of newborn clothes.
  • A big set of bottles before you know how feeding is going.
  • Gadgets like wipe warmers. A baby monitor depends on the size of your home.

How to save money

  • Ask friends and family for hand-me-down clothes and gear.
  • Wait until after your baby shower to buy big items. People often give the same things.
  • Compare prices and read the return policy before you order.
